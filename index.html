Your first engineering project is a lamp.

Lampwright takes you from an idea to a working lamp you made yourself. Shape it on screen, 3D print it, then wire in a switch: a push button, a touch sensor, or our favourite, a dimmer you work by twisting the shade.

Three ways to switch it on

Twist to dim

Our favourite. The shade itself is the knob: turn it, and two printed gears move a potentiometer that sets how bright the lamp glows.

Push to switch

A button in the side of the base clicks the lamp on and off.

Tap to switch

A touch sensor hidden behind the wall of the base. It runs from a battery, so there is no cord.

Three stages, one working lamp

  1. Design

    Pick a starting form and change it with sliders: height, width, ribs, cut-outs. The model updates as you move them, and the app warns you when something will not print or the bulb will not fit.

  2. Print

    Download the parts for your 3D printer. The app tells you how much plastic you need, roughly how long it takes, and which parts need supports.

  3. Build

    Fit the bulb holder and wire in a switch: a twist dimmer, a push button or a touch sensor. Each ready-made lamp comes with a shopping guide and step-by-step instructions.

What you pick up along the way

  • How designers change a shape with numbers instead of redrawing it
  • Why printed parts need gaps to fit together, and how big those gaps are
  • How two gears trade a small twist for a big turn
  • What a potentiometer is and how a circuit reads it
  • How to read a parts list and wire a safe, low-voltage circuit

For teachers and club leaders

  • It runs in a web browser. There is nothing to install and no account to make.
  • Each design saves as a small file a student can hand in or share.
  • The dimmer runs on a 12 V plug-in adapter, so nobody wires mains voltage.
  • You need a 3D printer and a handful of low-voltage parts.

      Starting form

      Cut-outs are holes through the shade. Their top edges print as short bridges, so keep them small for a clean result.

      Skin is a fine texture on the outside of the shade. It makes a much denser model, so files are larger.

      Socket hole sizes are typical values. Measure your own lamp holder before printing. Use LED bulbs only: printed plastic softens with heat.
